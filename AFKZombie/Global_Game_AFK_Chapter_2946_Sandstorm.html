Chapter 2946

Sandstorm

From original Chinese Chapter 2947 · 沙尘

Captain Giselle was beginning to regret coming.

He had underestimated the mission's difficulty.

Fang Heng might be immensely powerful inside the game, but they were outside it now.

In an environment this complicated and hostile, even he might be powerless.

"No rush. I can leave the outer reconnaissance to my summoned creatures. I'll see if we can find somewhere with weaker sandstorms and park the ship there to reduce energy consumption..."

Fang Heng stopped mid-sentence, a smile appearing on his lips.

"Wait. I think I've found something interesting."

Captain Giselle had been about to lead his men back aboard, but he paused and looked toward the swirling sand in the distance with Fang Heng.

Several shapes soon emerged from the storm.

Zerg!

Three Lickers burst out of the blowing sand and came up to Fang Heng. They opened their mouths and extended their curled tongues, revealing dark-golden chunks of ore.

Several nearby researchers saw the ore, and their expressions changed abruptly.

Mixed ore?

"Mr. Fang Heng, may I examine those pieces?"

After Fang Heng nodded, a researcher stepped forward cautiously and used tweezers to place a small fragment into a sealed bag.

Captain Giselle asked, "Is it rare ore?"

"Yes. Judging from its appearance, it's quite possibly rare Mutated Tungsten Ore. We'll need more tests to determine its exact alloy composition, but the concentration looks promising."

The surrounding players grew visibly excited.

Mutated Tungsten Ore could be used to produce metal with self-repairing properties and enhanced defensive strength.

It was valuable both for scientific research and for exchanging directly with the Kinsfolk for Contribution Points.

If they could establish themselves here and collect enough ore, the rewards would be enormous.

"Good. Take it for a full examination."

Fang Heng checked his game log.

The Lickers had found the ore in an open-pit deposit exposed to the sandstorm.

Apart from the blowing sand, they had detected no abnormal life forms or threats nearby.

Excellent.

That made things simple.

He could summon zombie clones and start mining immediately.

This time he'd come prepared. The ship carried crates of entrenching shovels—more than enough for his zombie clones.

Its auxiliary compartments could perform preliminary ore sorting. After processing through alchemy, tempering techniques, and Kinsfolk ore-refining equipment, the resulting Mutated Steel could be graded and submitted for Kinsfolk Contribution Points.

The dozen or so presidential experts looked excited too and gathered to whisper among themselves.

Captain Giselle asked, "Boss Fang, can your summoned creatures handle the mining?"

"Mm. Shouldn't be a problem. Arrange the work and I'll find a way to transport the ore into the ship."

"Excellent. We'll receive the ore aboard the ship, assign personnel to sorting, screening, refining, and the rest, and designate construction zones for the base..."

...

A little over ten minutes later, Lickers began making repeated trips through the sandstorm, returning to the ship and spitting out metal chunks.

The research team aboard immediately began sorting and grading the ore.

Watching through the ship's windows, the experts proposed, "Captain Giselle, transporting ore with summoned creatures is terribly inefficient. Could we use carts?"

"No. The sandstorms here contain corrosive gases. Even alloy containers will gradually be damaged and destroyed. For now, we have to rely on Mr. Fang Heng's summoned creatures to collect the ore."

Fang Heng glanced at the experts chattering away and felt an inexplicable surge of irritation.

They had wasted time asking questions throughout the journey.

And most of their questions concerned trivial details.

He couldn't be bothered answering them.

He genuinely admired Captain Giselle's patience in putting up with the lot of them.

The mining operation was slow at the moment only because he hadn't summoned enough Lickers yet.

Once the full swarm had arrived, he intended to handle mining and transportation himself, leaving sorting, grading, and refining to the Federation.

That division of labor would maximize efficiency.

But the experts weren't finished. "Captain Giselle, could you send a squad to escort us to the site where the summoned creatures are working?"

Giselle felt a headache coming on.

Seriously? What was wrong with these people?

Staying aboard the ship was the safest option under these circumstances.

Did they want to go out and get themselves killed?

Unfortunately, they had been appointed directly by the President's Office, so Giselle couldn't simply rebuff them. "That would be difficult. Our protective suits can't last very long in these conditions."

The experts glanced at Fang Heng and the others. "Please find a way to accommodate us, Captain. The summoned creatures aren't subject to Federation oversight. That's unfair to the Federation."

One of them turned to Fang Heng. "What do you think, Mr. Fang?"

"Unfair? What oversight do you want?" Fang Heng gave them a cold look. "Why don't I hand you control of my summoned creatures? You can run the operation yourselves."

The experts considered the suggestion. "That would work too."

Unbelievable.

Did these so-called experts seriously want to order his Lickers around?

Fang Heng was speechless. He decided they weren't worth another moment of his time.

Watching from the side, Wayne and the others struggled not to laugh.

Were these experts out of their minds?

They wanted to tell Boss Fang how to do his job?

Boss Fang was doing the Federation a favor by letting them take part, and now they were taking a mile after being given an inch!

Seeing the atmosphere grow tense, Captain Giselle rubbed his aching temples.

He was used to dealing with these presidential "experts." He patiently explained, "Ladies and gentlemen, there's no need to talk about 'oversight.' Cooperation depends on trust. The military trusts Mr. Fang Heng. We can't contribute much to improving efficiency, so we'll leave that to Boss Fang. If there's anything we can help with, please don't hesitate to ask."

Hearing Giselle back down, the experts looked dissatisfied and resumed whispering among themselves.

"You're too kind, Captain. I'll go check on the transport operation." Fang Heng waved a hand. "Wayne, let's go outside and take a look."
